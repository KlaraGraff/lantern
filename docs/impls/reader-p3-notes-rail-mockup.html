Chapter Eight

Another Way into Orgoreyn

It is a durable, quietly mysterious kind of loyalty. We crossed the border in darkness, with no witness but the snow and the small blue lamps of the sledge.

The first hours were passed in silence. Estraven had said that fear was useful only when it made the senses exact; all other fear was borrowed from tomorrow.

By noon the mountains behind us had become a pale wall. I could no longer tell whether I was leaving Karhide or carrying it with me. A border persists most stubbornly in the mind of the person who has crossed it.

He spoke then, not looking at me. “Patriotism is not love. It is the fear of the other. I have tried to learn the difference.”

Nothing in his tone asked agreement. The statement lay between us like another item of equipment: light enough to carry, dangerous to forget.

I wrote it down that evening. The act seemed absurdly formal, but I understood that memory changes an argument into a mood, and a mood is much harder to examine.

The road curved north. In the failing light the ice fields appeared not empty but complete, a country whose language we had not yet earned.
