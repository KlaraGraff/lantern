Chapter Four

The Power of Three

Language is built from patterns, and the most satisfying pattern is often the simplest. Three examples feel complete without becoming predictable, while a longer procession can lose its energy before reaching the end.

Writers have understood this instinctively for centuries. They promise blood, sweat and tears; they speak of life, liberty and happiness; they arrange an argument so that each phrase adds weight to the last.

The effect is not merely decorative. A balanced sentence makes a difficult idea easier to follow, because rhythm quietly tells the reader where one thought finishes and another begins.
